    04 X-Ray Vision · Debugging code

    In the whole routing code, points come off one way and go back on one way.

    1. Who's free nearbyavailability.py
    2. Put them in order 60% distance · 25% said yes lately · 15% skillsrouting.py
    3. Buzz the top person 60 s to answer (was 90)offer.py
    4. Yes: +8 · anything else: −12 a miss counts as a nohistory.py
    5. Next person down first yes winsoffer.py
    What it means

    Nothing puts points back with time. Wen asked about this in 2019, and it was "left as-is". From zero, it takes ~13 yeses in a row to get level, and every yes needs an offer that someone at the bottom rarely gets.

    Marcus's 14 Aug question, answered

    Was the change meant for responders who keep turning jobs down? No. It fell out that way. Before 4.2, Vesper had the 2nd-best record of all 16. The four shared one bad release week (36–50% taken), not a habit.

    Correction: 4.2's reweight softened a low score's effect (~40 → ~19 min of travel "penalty"). The likely trigger is the 60 s timer. The root cause is older scoring that can't recover.
    05 Super-Speed · Building without coding

    Helen asked for more than a number change. Back in Rotation is what we'd build instead.

    Three pieces, shipped together
    • Being quiet gets you asked. Available but no offer in 7 days: next matching callout comes to you first.
    • A bad stretch fades with time. Scores drift back toward neutral.
    • Missing isn't refusing. A timeout costs less than a decline.
    Deliberately doesn't
    • undo 4.2 or change the 60 s timer
    • equalise workload or set quotas
    • override someone choosing to step back
    • add a setting, or touch the availability record that Supply reads
    What changes for people

    Kip stops telling Mite to "hang in there", because the card says why and what happens next. Meteor Mite sees "You're back in rotation" instead of silence.

    06 Sidekicks · Building your own skills

    My review habits, written once as a skill and run on every brief.

    review-checklist: five checks
    • Named owner, a person, not a team
    • Created date and time
    • How we'll know it worked
    • Scope at the end matches the start
    • Problem explained before the fix
    Run on my own brief
    2 / 5 → 5 / 5

    It caught the missing owner, the missing time, and "three pieces" meaning three different things in three places.
